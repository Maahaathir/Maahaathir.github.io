## The Idea

The Local Group is a collection of more than 120 galaxies, mostly dwarf galaxies, defined primarily by its two giants: the **Milky Way** and **Andromeda**. It is a galactic neighborhood sitting inside the **Virgo Supercluster** and spanning roughly 10 million light-years across (previously, astronomers thought it stretched across 23 million light-years).

All of these galaxies are gravitationally bound and travel through the universe together. At the center of attention sit the two large spiral galaxies: Andromeda takes the crown as the largest, while our home sits in second place (not even first in this, TwT). Edwin Hubble first introduced the term *"The Local Group"* in Chapter VI of his 1936 book, *The Realm of the Nebulae*.

## A Cosmic Collision and Dark Matter Clues

Because these galaxies are gravitationally bound, they are predicted to merge into one giant elliptical galaxy in the distant future. Andromeda is currently moving toward the Milky Way at roughly **300 km/s** relative to the Sun (don't worry, even your great-great-grandchildren are safe, not sure about AI taking over, though).

I personally find this region deeply intriguing. As our engineering and observational instruments improve, studying this small corner of the cosmos with high precision gives us a benchmark for places humans will never reach, we aren't even getting out of this galaxy anytime soon, and look at the scales I'm comparing, haha. On top of that, measuring the total mass of the Local Group offers one of our best windows into unveiling the nature of **dark matter**. I don't know how much I will learn about the Local Group in my lifetime, but it certainly holds answers to some of the universe's biggest mysteries.
